# Change of Character (CHOCH)

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What traders mean by a change of character, and how it differs from a break of structure.
- Why definitions of CHOCH vary between traders, and why you must fix your own definition in writing.
- Why a CHOCH is a warning to reassess rather than proof that a trend has reversed.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

A change of character, often written CHOCH, is a term some traders use to describe the first sign that
price behaviour has changed. In an uptrend, the character of the market is that price keeps making higher
highs and higher lows, and each pullback holds above the previous low. A CHOCH is said to occur when
price breaks a swing low against that pattern — the first time in a while that the market has done
something the uptrend said it should not do. In a downtrend, the mirror image applies: price breaks a
swing high.

The important thing to understand up front is that definitions vary. Different traders draw the CHOCH
line at different points: some require the break of the most recent swing low, some the swing low that
started the current leg, some a candle close beyond it, some simply a wick. There is no single
authoritative definition. What matters is not which definition you pick, but that you pick one, write it
down, and apply it consistently.

This is what separates a CHOCH from a break of structure. A BOS confirms that the existing sequence is
still running. A CHOCH marks the first break against that sequence. It is a smaller, earlier, and much
less certain event. It is best understood as a warning light rather than a verdict: something in the
behaviour of price has changed, and the honest response is to slow down and reassess, not to flip
direction immediately.

That distinction matters because a CHOCH happens frequently inside trends that then continue. An uptrend
can break a minor swing low, pull back, and then resume higher with a new sequence of higher highs. If
you treated every CHOCH as a reversal signal, you would spend most of your time positioned against the
prevailing move. The label describes a change in short-term behaviour; it does not establish that the
larger trend is over.

The practical use of a CHOCH is organisational. It tells you to stop assuming the old pattern still
applies, to identify which levels now matter, and to wait for additional evidence before committing to a
direction. Waiting costs you nothing. Acting on a warning light as though it were proof is how traders
end up on the wrong side of a trend that never actually turned.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to demonstrate the labelling. Suppose you are looking at
USD/JPY on the fifteen-minute chart during a London session. Price has been climbing all morning, with a
series of higher highs and higher lows: a low at 149.20, a high at 149.85, a low at 149.50, and a high at
150.10.

According to your written rule, a CHOCH occurs when a fifteen-minute candle closes below the most recent
swing low at 149.50. At 11:15, a candle closes at 149.42. That is a change of character by your
definition, and you note it.

What you do next is the substance of the lesson. You do not sell. You write down that the bullish
character of the morning has been interrupted, you mark 150.10 as the high that would need to be exceeded
for the old sequence to resume, and you mark the next lower high as the level that would suggest a genuine
shift is developing. Then you wait.

The example does not tell you what happened afterwards, because nothing about a CHOCH predicts it. Price
might have continued down and built a full downtrend. It might equally have dipped to 149.35, found
buyers, and pushed back above 150.10 within the hour, leaving the original uptrend intact and the CHOCH
looking, in hindsight, like an ordinary pullback. Both outcomes happen regularly, which is exactly why a
CHOCH is a prompt to reassess rather than a signal to reverse.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Treating a CHOCH as a confirmed reversal and immediately taking the opposite position.
- Using a different definition of CHOCH on each chart, so no two analyses are comparable.
- Applying a CHOCH on a timeframe you are not trading and acting on a different chart's structure.
- Ignoring the CHOCH entirely and continuing to buy into a trend that has visibly lost its character.
- Confusing the first counter-trend break with a full trend change, then over-sizing the trade.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Treat a change of character as a reason to stop and reassess, never as permission to reverse your
position.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing
you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Write your CHOCH definition in one sentence, stating which swing must break and whether a close or a wick
is required. Then find four historical examples where price broke against its short-term trend, and for
each one record: the pair, the timeframe, the level broken, and the time of the candle.

Next, follow each of the four forward on the chart and classify what happened afterwards in neutral
language — either "the prior sequence resumed" or "a new sequence developed". Do not score yourself on
whether you would have profited; score yourself only on whether you identified the level correctly under
your own rule.

Finally, write down what additional evidence, beyond the CHOCH itself, you would want before changing
your directional bias. Keep that list to three items or fewer.

*Why this matters:* the gap between "behaviour changed" and "trend reversed" is where most impulsive
trades are born, and separating the two in writing is the discipline this lesson exists to build.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** An uptrend on the one-hour chart prints a low at 149.50, and a candle later closes at 149.42,
below it. Under the definition given in this lesson, what has occurred?

- A. A confirmed trend reversal to the downside.
- B. A change of character, which is a warning to reassess rather than proof of reversal.
- C. A break of structure confirming the uptrend is still intact.
- D. Nothing, because only a wick below the low would count.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** A break against the prevailing short-term sequence, on a close beyond the most recent swing low,
is a change of character — an early warning that definitions vary on, not a confirmed reversal.
