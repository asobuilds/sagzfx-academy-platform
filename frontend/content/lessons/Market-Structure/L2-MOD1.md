# Higher Highs, Higher Lows, LH & LL

## 1. What You'll Learn

- How to mark meaningful swing highs and swing lows instead of reacting to every small bump.
- What higher highs and higher lows describe, and what lower highs and lower lows describe.
- Why one swing never proves a trend, and how to say clearly when structure is unclear.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Market structure is the sequence of meaningful swing highs and swing lows a chart produces. Before you can
label anything, you need a rule for what counts as a swing. A swing high is a candle high with lower highs
on both sides of it. A swing low is a candle low with higher lows on both sides. The word meaningful is
doing real work there: you ignore the small bumps that appear on every timeframe and mark only the turns
that visibly changed price direction. Whatever rule you choose, write it down and apply it the same way
every time, because your labels change if your definition of a swing changes halfway across the chart.

Once the swings are marked, the labels are simple. In an uptrend, price prints a higher high (HH), which
is a high above the previous swing high, and a higher low (HL), which is a low above the previous swing
low. In a downtrend, price prints a lower high (LH) and a lower low (LL). Notice that each label is a
comparison with the swing before it. Structure is a sequence, not a single event. One higher high on its
own tells you very little, because price can spike and give the move back immediately.

The third possibility matters just as much: a range. When price makes roughly equal highs and roughly
equal lows, the sequence has no direction, and labelling a range as a trend is one of the most common
beginner errors. If you cannot say which swing was broken most recently, the honest answer is that
structure is unclear.

There is a second honesty point. Different timeframes can show different structures at the same moment. A
daily chart may be in an uptrend while the fifteen-minute chart is in a clean downtrend. Both statements
can be true, because they describe different windows of time. This is why you always name the timeframe
first. "On the four-hour chart, structure is bullish" is a checkable claim. "The market is bullish" is not.

Finally, understand what these labels can and cannot do. Labelling structure tells you what price has
already done. It does not tell you what price will do next. A clean sequence of higher highs and higher
lows can end on the very next candle, and it frequently does. Treat structure as a description of the past
that helps you organise a chart, not as a prediction.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to show you how the labelling works. Suppose you are looking at
GBP/USD on the four-hour chart during a London session. Over several weeks, price has printed the
following swing points: a low at 1.2600, a high at 1.2750, a low at 1.2660, a high at 1.2840, and a low
at 1.2740.

Working through the sequence: the high at 1.2750 is above nothing yet, so you mark it and wait. The low at
1.2660 is above the previous low at 1.2600, so it is a higher low. The high at 1.2840 is above the previous
high at 1.2750, so it is a higher high. The low at 1.2740 is above the previous low at 1.2660, so it is
another higher low. You now have HH, HL, HH, HL on the four-hour chart, and you can write one sentence
describing it.

What you cannot do is conclude that price will continue higher. The next swing might be a lower high at
1.2800, which would be the first sign that the sequence is stalling, and it might be followed by a break of
the 1.2740 low. Sequences of this shape fail regularly. A disciplined trader writes the description, marks
the low at 1.2740 as the level that would invalidate the bullish reading, and then waits rather than
assuming the pattern must continue.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Marking every minor high and low as a swing, so the chart becomes a wall of labels with no meaning.
- Calling a trend after a single higher high, instead of waiting for the sequence to develop.
- Switching timeframe mid-analysis because a different chart supports the trade you already wanted.
- Describing a range as an uptrend or downtrend when highs and lows are roughly equal.
- Treating a structure label as a forecast, then holding a losing position because "the trend is up".

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Name the timeframe and mark the level that would prove your structure read wrong before you consider a
single entry.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. This account uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and
nothing you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose three pairs and one timeframe for each — for example EUR/USD on the four-hour, GBP/USD on the
daily, and USD/JPY on the one-hour. On each chart, mark the last five clear swing highs and the last five
clear swing lows. Write down your swing rule first, in one sentence, so you can check later whether you
applied it consistently.

Then label each swing HH, HL, LH or LL, and write a single sentence describing the current structure on
that chart. If the highs and lows are roughly equal, write "range" instead of forcing a direction. Finish
by naming, for each chart, the exact price level that would make your description wrong.

*Why this matters:* the level that invalidates your read is the same level you will later use for stop
placement, so this exercise builds the habit of defining structure and risk in the same pass.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** On the four-hour chart, price prints a high at 1.2750, a low at 1.2660, a high at 1.2840,
then a low at 1.2740. The prior swing low was 1.2600. Which description is most accurate?

- A. A confirmed uptrend that will continue until the daily chart agrees.
- B. A sequence of higher highs and higher lows, which describes the recent past and can still fail.
- C. A range, because no swing low has been broken.
- D. A downtrend beginning, because the last move was downward from 1.2840 to 1.2740.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** The high at 1.2840 exceeded the prior high and the low at 1.2740 held above the prior low, which
is a higher high and a higher low — a description of what has happened, not a forecast.
