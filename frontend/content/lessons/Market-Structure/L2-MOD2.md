# Break of Structure (BOS)

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What a break of structure is, and which swing level has to break for it to count.
- Why you must decide in advance whether a wick or a candle close counts as the break.
- Why breaks fail, and how to state the invalidation for a break before you act on it.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

A break of structure, usually shortened to BOS, describes price moving beyond a meaningful prior swing
level. In an uptrend, that means price trading above the most recent swing high. In a downtrend, it means
price trading below the most recent swing low. The idea is simple: as long as price keeps breaking the
swings in its direction, the sequence you learned in the previous lesson is still intact. When it stops
breaking them, something has changed and you should reassess.

The first decision you have to make is which swing level matters. Not every high on the chart is a
meaningful swing high. Use the same swing rule you applied when labelling structure, and pick the most
recent clear swing that the market actually respected. If you find yourself choosing between three
candidate levels after the move has already happened, that is a sign the level was never clearly defined
in the first place.

The second decision is what counts as a break. Some traders require a candle to close beyond the level;
others count a wick that trades through it. Neither rule is wrong, but they produce different results, and
mixing them is how a trader ends up counting breaks that were never breaks. Pick one, write it down, and
apply it to every chart the same way: a close-based rule gives fewer signals and more confirmation, while a
wick-based rule gives earlier signals and more false ones.

The third thing to understand is that breaks fail. This is not a rare malfunction; it is normal market
behaviour. Price can trade beyond a level, fail to attract follow-through, and return inside the range.
When that happens, the level has not been invalidated as a level — your interpretation of what the break
meant has. Every break should therefore come with a stated invalidation: the price at which you would
accept that the break did not hold.

Be careful about one more thing. A BOS is a description of price behaviour, not a signal. Knowing that
price broke a swing high tells you the prior sequence was extended. It does not tell you to buy, and it
does not tell you how far price will travel.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These numbers are hypothetical and are used only to show the mechanics. Suppose you are watching EUR/USD
on the one-hour chart during a New York session. Price has been rising, with a recent swing high at
1.0920 and a swing low at 1.0875.

At 14:00, a one-hour candle closes at 1.0926, which is above the 1.0920 swing high. Under a close-based
rule, that is a break of structure to the upside. You note the level, and you note that the candle body
closed beyond it rather than merely wicking through.

Now define the invalidation before doing anything else. If the bullish reading depends on that break
holding, then a close back below 1.0920 would weaken it, and a close below the 1.0875 swing low would
invalidate the sequence entirely. Those two levels are your reference points, and they exist whether or
not you ever take a trade.

What this example does not tell you is what happened next. Price might have continued higher, and it might
equally have reversed back below 1.0920 within the hour, turning the break into a failed one. Breaks of
this shape fail often enough that treating any single break as reliable is a mistake. The discipline being
taught here is not predicting the outcome; it is naming the level, naming the rule you used to identify
the break, and naming the price at which you would admit the break did not hold.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Counting a wick through a level as a break on one trade and requiring a close on the next, depending on
  which answer suits the position you already hold.
- Choosing the swing level after the move, so the break is defined by hindsight rather than by rule.
- Treating a BOS as a buy or sell signal instead of a description of what price has done.
- Failing to state an invalidation, then holding a position after the break has clearly failed.
- Marking breaks on a timeframe you do not actually trade, then acting on a different chart entirely.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

A break of structure is a statement about the past, so define the price that would prove it failed before
you act on it.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing
you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Write down your break rule in one sentence — either "a candle must close beyond the level" or "a wick
through the level counts". Then find five historical breaks of structure on charts of your choice and
record, for each one: the pair, the timeframe, the exact swing level broken, the time of the candle that
broke it, and whether the break met your rule.

Now go back through the same five examples and find the first case where price returned to the broken
level. Record what happened at the retest in neutral language, without deciding whether the break
"succeeded". Finally, for each of the five, write the invalidation level you would have used.

*Why this matters:* recording breaks and their failures side by side, using one fixed rule, is the fastest
way to see that a break is evidence rather than a guarantee, and it builds the habit of defining
invalidation at the same moment you identify the setup.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** You use a close-based break rule. Price wicks above a swing high at 1.0920 but the one-hour
candle closes at 1.0914. What is the correct reading under your stated rule?

- A. A valid break of structure, because price traded above the level.
- B. A failed break, so the uptrend has reversed.
- C. No break under your rule, because no candle closed beyond 1.0920.
- D. A break, but only if the next candle also trades above 1.0920.

**Correct answer:** C

**Why:** Under a close-based rule the break requires a candle close beyond the level, and the close at
1.0914 is below it — consistency with your written rule matters more than which rule you chose.
