# Inducement

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What inducement is, and why an obvious level often draws early entries before the level that matters.
- How to tell an inducement from a genuine entry area using evidence on the chart rather than hindsight.
- How inducement sits in relation to the order block you have already marked.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Inducement is a name for the pullback or level that arrives before the one you are actually waiting for. It
is the minor pause, the small obvious low, the first touch of a zone — offered before price reaches the area
your plan was built around. The concept describes a
predictable problem in how markets travel: price rarely moves in a straight line from one zone to the next,
and the stops and entries created along the way are themselves resting orders that have to be dealt with.

Suppose you have marked a demand zone on the four-hour chart — the origin
of the move that broke structure upward — and you intend to look for longs if price returns there. Between
current price and that zone there is usually a minor higher low: a small swing low that formed on the way up.
Traders who want to buy will see it as an obvious place
to enter, because it is the most recent point price turned up from. Both groups place orders at the same
small area. If price trades below that area first, the early entries are stopped out, and the level they were
watching is now spent. That is inducement: an obvious, minor level sitting between you and the zone that
actually matters.

The word covers two related things. First, inducement as a level: a minor swing low or high too shallow, too
recent, or too obvious to be the origin of anything larger. Second, inducement as an area: a first touch of
an order block that arrives before the deeper part of the zone, encouraging an entry at a price you did not
plan for.

How do you tell inducement from a genuine entry before the outcome is known? Was this level the origin of a
structural break, or just a pause inside a leg? Is it at
premium or discount relative to the range you are trading? Is there an imbalance between it and the price you
expect to travel toward? Did price reach it with a decisive move or with a slow drift? A genuine zone is one
where several of those line up; an inducement usually answers them weakly, being where price hesitated rather
than changed direction.

The practical value is patience. If you expect inducement to sit between you and your zone, you stop treating
the first obvious level as the last chance to enter. You wait for the level your plan named, and accept that
if price never reaches it, the correct outcome is no trade.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose it is a Monday in the New York
session and you are on the fifteen-minute chart of GBP/USD, with the one-hour chart marked first.

On the one-hour chart you have marked a demand zone from 1.2640 to 1.2655. That zone is where a strong upward
move began, and that move broke the previous one-hour high at 1.2690. Your plan is to look for longs if price
returns into that zone.

Price is currently at 1.2720 and drifting lower. On the way down it stalls twice at 1.2688 — a minor swing
low barely ten pips below where the last up leg started. It is obvious, recent, and the first place a buyer
would think of. Your written invalidation sits at 1.2632, beneath the zone.

Is 1.2688 the area you planned to trade? It is not. It is a pause inside
the leg, not the origin of the breakout, and it does not sit in the deeper part of the move's range. It has
the shape of inducement: an obvious minor level between current price and the zone that your structure
actually named.

Notice what the example does not tell you. Price might have stalled at 1.2688 and turned up without
ever returning to your zone, in which case the correct output of your plan was no trade — and it might
equally have fallen straight through both the inducement and the zone.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Entering the first obvious pullback because waiting feels like missing out.
- Calling a level inducement only after price has passed it, when no decision was required.
- Marking an order block without checking for a smaller, more obvious swing in front.
- Treating every minor level as inducement, so no entry is ever taken.
- Moving the planned zone deeper to justify skipping the level that mattered.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

If a level is obvious enough that everyone can see it, ask what it is doing between you and the zone your
plan actually named.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing you
do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one pair and one timeframe. Find five past examples where an order block or demand zone you would have
marked was approached after a smaller, more obvious swing level formed in front of it. For each, mark the
inducement level in one colour and the zone in another, and record the distance in pips between them.

Then write two lines per example. First, state the evidence that made the zone the genuine area rather than
the level in front of it — a structural break, premium or discount, an imbalance. Second, write the sentence
you would have needed to say to yourself in order to stay out of the first level.

*Why this matters:* naming the inducement in advance, in writing, is the only way to tell later whether you
waited for your plan or simply followed the most obvious level on the chart and called it analysis
afterwards.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** You have marked a demand zone on the four-hour chart as the area you plan to trade. On the way
down, price forms an obvious minor higher low a short distance above the zone, then trades below it. What is
the most useful reading of that minor level?

- A. It is the genuine entry area, and the deeper zone should be ignored.
- B. It is likely inducement: a minor, obvious level that sits between current price and the zone the
  structure actually named, and it should be separated from the planned area using evidence such as
  structural origin, premium or discount, and imbalance.
- C. It proves the demand zone has failed and the plan is void.
- D. It confirms price will continue lower indefinitely.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** Inducement is identified by its position and character — minor, obvious, and in front of the planned
zone — not by what price does afterwards, so it is separated from a genuine area using evidence rather than
hindsight.
