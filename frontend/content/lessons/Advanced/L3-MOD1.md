# Advanced Market Structure

## 1. What You'll Learn

- How to separate major swings from minor swings using one written rule rather than a feeling.
- What internal and external structure describe, and how the two can point in different directions at once.
- How to name the specific level that would invalidate your current reading of the market.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

In earlier tiers you learned to read structure as a sequence of swing highs and swing lows. What changes at
this level is that you stop treating every swing as equally important. A chart contains swings of many
sizes, nested inside one another, and the first professional skill is deciding which ones you are actually
trading.

The tool for that is a written swing definition. A swing high is not simply "a place where price went up
and then down". It is a candle whose high is higher than the highs of a fixed number of candles on each
side of it. You choose that number, write it down, and apply it without exception. Two traders using
different definitions will draw different charts from the same data, and neither is wrong — but a trader
who changes definitions depending on the answer they want is not analysing, they are rationalising.

Once swings are defined consistently, you can group them. Major swings are the larger movements that
define the broad direction of the market over the period you are studying. Minor swings are the smaller
movements that make up each major swing. This creates two layers of structure, and they are usually
described as external and internal. External structure is the outer skeleton: the major highs and lows
that define the trend. Internal structure is the movement inside the current major leg, and it can
genuinely contradict the outer one.

They see the internal structure turn and declare
the trend over, when the external structure has not been touched. Or they hold a position because the
external structure is intact, ignoring that the internal structure is deteriorating and the pullback is
deeper than their plan allowed for. Both errors come from the same root: not stating in advance which
layer governs the decision.

The market also spends a great deal of time doing neither of the two clean things. It ranges. A range is
not the absence of structure; it is a different structure, defined by a boundary high and a boundary low
that price repeatedly respects. Transitions matter too. The shift from trend to range, and from range back
to trend, is where most of the money is lost, because the rules that worked in the previous regime are
applied to the next one.

Finally, every structural reading is provisional. It stays valid until a named level is broken. The
professional habit is not to be right about direction, but to be able to say precisely: this reading
stands while price holds above this level, and it is wrong below it.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose you are studying GBP/USD on
the four-hour chart across three weeks. You use a written swing rule of three candles on each side.

Your major swings show a sequence of higher highs and higher lows from 1.2600 up to a high at 1.2850,
then a pullback to 1.2740, which is a higher low against the previous 1.2680. On the major layer, this
still reads as an uptrend. External structure is intact.

Now you drop to the one-hour chart inside that most recent leg up from 1.2740 to 1.2850. There you see
the internal structure turn: the last minor high at 1.2835 is broken to the downside, and price makes a
lower high at 1.2820 before easing to 1.2790. Internal structure now leans down while external structure
leans up.

What this example establishes is the invalidation level, not a prediction. You would write: the uptrend
reading stands while the higher low at 1.2740 holds; a four-hour close below 1.2740 invalidates it and
puts the market into a range or a reversal, which must then be assessed on its own evidence.

Notice what the example does not tell you. It does not say price went on to 1.3000, and it does not say it
reversed. The internal structure turning down is a warning to reduce expectations
and tighten your invalidation, not a signal to sell.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Changing your swing definition when a trade is going against you.
- Treating an internal pullback as a trend change before external structure breaks.
- Marking structure without fixing the timeframe first.
- Declaring a range "no structure" and then forcing trend rules onto it.
- Describing a structural view without ever naming the price level that would prove it wrong.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Define your swing rule once, write it down, and let a named price level — not your opinion — decide when
your reading of structure has failed.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing
you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one major pair and one timeframe. Write your swing definition as an exact rule — for example,
"a swing high is a candle whose high exceeds the highs of the three candles on each side". Then annotate
one month of that chart twice: once marking major swings and once marking the minor swings inside them,
using two different colours.

Below the chart, write three sentences. First, state the current external structure in one line. Second,
state the current internal structure in one line. Third, name the single price level that would invalidate
your external reading, and say what you would do if price closed beyond it.

*Why this matters:* writing the swing rule before annotating is what stops the definition from drifting to
fit the conclusion, and naming the invalidation level converts a vague opinion into a testable statement.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** On the four-hour chart, GBP/USD is making higher highs and higher lows on the major swings,
while the one-hour structure inside the latest leg has just broken a minor low and made a lower high.
What is the most accurate description of this situation?

- A. The trend has reversed and the correct action is to sell.
- B. External structure still reads as an uptrend while internal structure leans down, so the reading
  stands until the named higher low is broken.
- C. The chart is invalid and no analysis can be made until the two timeframes agree.
- D. Internal structure always overrides external structure, so the trend is now down.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** Major and minor swings describe different layers of the same market, so they can point in
opposite directions; the reading survives until a specific level is broken, and that level must be named
in advance.
