# Displacement

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What displacement means, and how it differs from an ordinary large candle.
- Why displacement gives a structure break evidential weight and how it leaves imbalance behind.
- How to judge displacement objectively using body size relative to range and recent average range.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Displacement is the name for a move that is forceful and one-directional rather than merely large. You have
met the idea already: when you learned to confirm a structure break, the textbook version said price must
close beyond the swing point. Displacement is the refinement of that idea — not just did price close beyond
the level, but how did it get there, and what did it leave behind?

Start with the plain definition. A displaced candle is one whose body is large relative to its total range,
moving decisively in one direction, with little of the candle given back to the opposite side. A candle that
opens at 1.0840, closes at 1.0862 and never trades below 1.0838 has a 22-pip body and a 24-pip range: almost
all of it is progress in one direction. A candle that opens at 1.0840, spikes to 1.0870 and closes at 1.0844
has a 28-pip range but a 4-pip body: effort in both directions, little net travel. The first is displacement. The second is a rejection, and it says something different
about where buyers and sellers were willing to trade.

The second part of the definition is comparison. A candle is not displaced in isolation but relative to
what the chart has recently been doing. You measure the recent average body size — say over the
last twenty candles — and compare. A body three times that average, closing near its extreme and breaking a
level, is a very different event from a candle of average size that happens to sit just past a swing high.

A break of structure carries more evidential weight when it
arrives with displacement. A level crossed by a body
three times the average, with the close near the extreme, tells you that aggressive orders consumed the
resting interest at that level quickly. That is the mechanical link between displacement and imbalance: when
price moves through a region fast, with a large body and little overlap, it tends to leave a gap or a thin
area between one candle and the next. The fair value gaps you learned to mark in the Market Structure tier
usually appear for exactly this reason.

So displacement is evidence, not a trade. It can confirm that a structure break deserves your attention, and
it can show you where an imbalance was left. It does not tell you that price will return to that imbalance,
or that the direction will continue. Those remain questions you answer with your plan and your invalidation
level.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose it is a Tuesday in the London
session and you are watching EUR/USD on the one-hour chart.

You have measured body size over the last twenty one-hour candles and found an average body of about 12 pips.
The recent structure has been a gentle drift lower, with the last swing low at 1.0815.

At 10:00 the one-hour candle opens at 1.0824 and closes at 1.0801, a 23-pip body, with its low at 1.0799, so
the close sits within two pips of the extreme. That body is roughly twice your recent average, and the candle
closes below the swing low at 1.0815.

Now you apply the comparison rather than reacting to the size alone. Twice the average is meaningful but not
extreme, and you would note that honestly in your journal rather than calling it textbook displacement. You
would also check whether the move left a gap: between the 10:00 high at 1.0826 and the previous candle's low
at 1.0822 there is no gap, so on this timeframe the move has not left a clean imbalance, though a lower
timeframe might show one.

What you have is evidence that the structure break arrived with some force, and a named level that has been
crossed. What you do not have is a direction for the next move. Price could have retraced immediately from
1.0801, and it could equally have continued. The displacement is one fact, correctly measured, and no more
than that.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Calling a candle displaced because it was large, without measuring its body against its range.
- Judging displacement by mood instead of a rolling average written in advance.
- Treating a slow, overlapping drift past a level as a forceful break.
- Assuming displacement guarantees continuation, or that price must return to the imbalance.
- Measuring displacement on a timeframe you are not trading, against the wrong average.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Measure displacement against a number you wrote down before the candle closed, or you will only ever see it
when you already want to.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing you
do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one pair and one timeframe. Pick a stretch of at least 100 candles and calculate the average body
size, in pips, of the first twenty. Then scan the rest of the chart for every candle that closes beyond a
recent swing point.

For each such break, record three numbers: the body size, the total range, and the body as a multiple of your
rolling twenty-candle average. Mark the breaks reaching at least twice the average in one colour and the
others in another, then note for each which strong breaks left a gap between candles and which did not.

*Why this matters:* writing the average down first and recording the multiple turns displacement into a
measurement you can compare across hundreds of examples, rather than a feeling that shifts according to what
you want the chart to say.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** Two candles each close below a recent swing low. Candle one has a 22-pip body and a 24-pip
range. Candle two has a 6-pip body and a 30-pip range. Using the definition in this lesson, which is
displaced?

- A. Candle two, because it has the larger total range.
- B. Both, because both closed beyond the swing low.
- C. Candle one, because its body is large relative to its range and moves decisively in one direction, while
  candle two shows effort in both directions with little net travel.
- D. Neither, because displacement requires a gap between candles.

**Correct answer:** C

**Why:** Displacement is measured by the body relative to the candle's own range — a large body with little
give-back shows one-directional force, whereas a wide range with a tiny body shows rejection rather than
displacement.
