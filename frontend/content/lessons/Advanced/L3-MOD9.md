# FVG + Liquidity Confluence

## 1. What You'll Learn

- How to check whether a fair value gap genuinely overlaps an order block instead of merely sitting near one.
- How to grade confluence with a written checklist rather than stacking labels until a chart looks convincing.
- Why swept liquidity and premium or discount position change what a gap is worth, and where the checklist stops being useful.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Confluence means several independent pieces of evidence pointing at the same place on the chart. The
three you already have from the Market Structure tier are the fair value gap, the order block, and
liquidity. A fair value gap is the thin area left when price travels quickly across three candles. An
order block is the last opposing candle before a displacement. Liquidity means the resting orders that
accumulate above a sequence of highs or below a sequence of lows, where traders place stops and pending
entries.

Confluence begins with a question: do these two zones actually overlap? If your bullish order block runs
from 0.6580 to 0.6584 and your fair value gap runs from 0.6585 to 0.6590, they are neighbours, not
confluence. If the gap runs from 0.6583 to 0.6588, the two share 0.6583 to 0.6584 — one pip of genuine
overlap. The first step of grading is arithmetic, not judgement.

The second ingredient is liquidity that has already been taken. A swept level is a recent high or low that
price has moved beyond and then left behind — the visible trace of resting orders having been filled at
that price. A zone with a swept level behind it has already consumed part of that interest.

The third is where the zone sits in the range. Premium and discount are the two halves of a dealing range
divided at its midpoint: the upper half is premium, where price is comparatively expensive, and the lower
half is discount, where it is comparatively cheap. A bullish zone in discount, with a swept low behind it
and a gap overlapping its edge, has three reasons to be watched. A bullish zone in premium with no sweep
nearby has one.

Where this goes wrong is when the checklist becomes a garnish: a trader who needs a setup finds a gap,
then a block near it, then some high or low callable as a sweep, and declares four-out-of-four. The defence
is to fix the checklist, fix its order, and write the score down before you decide. A checklist run after
forming a view cannot correct the view.

Be explicit about what the score means: a high score describes how much independent evidence sits in one
place, not a probability. Zones with excellent confluence are traded through regularly, and four out of
four is still a setup that can fail.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose it is a New York session and
you are watching AUD/USD on the fifteen-minute chart.

A dealing range has formed between a low at 0.6572 and a high at 0.6640, midpoint 0.6606. Below the range,
price has recently traded down to 0.6569 — three pips under the 0.6572 low — before moving back up. That
is your swept level, sitting behind the current move.

Inside the range there is a bullish order block from 0.6576 to 0.6584, and an upward fair value gap from
0.6580 to 0.6588. The two overlap between 0.6580 and 0.6584, a four-pip band. The zone sits entirely below
0.6606, so it is in discount.

Run the checklist in a fixed order. Does a fair value gap overlap the block? Yes, four pips. Is a level
behind the zone swept? Yes, 0.6572 was taken and price returned above it. Is the zone in discount? Yes.
Is the zone fresh, or already tested? That is the fourth question, answered from the chart rather than
assumed.

Suppose the zone scores three of four because it has already been partly tested. The checklist has done
its job — it produced a grade and a written reason for the missing point. Price might react from 0.6580 to
0.6588, and it might close straight through 0.6576. Both are ordinary. A four-of-four score would not
change that, which is why the invalidation level, not the score, is what governs the decision.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Calling two zones confluent when they are adjacent but share no prices.
- Running the checklist backwards from the trade you want, finding labels to support it.
- Treating a confluence score as a probability, and sizing up because the score is high.
- Drawing a dealing range after the fact so the zone lands in discount instead of checking where it sits.
- Counting the same evidence twice — scoring a swept level and the gap it left as two points when they are one event.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Write the checklist, run it in a fixed order, and record the score before you allow yourself to decide what you want the chart to do.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing
you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one pair and one timeframe. Write your confluence checklist as four numbered questions in a fixed
order: overlap between a fair value gap and an order block, a swept level behind the zone, position in
premium or discount, and whether the zone is still fresh.

Then find ten zones and score each one out of four, recording the numbers behind each point — the pip
band of overlap, the swept price, the dealing range high, low and midpoint. Where you award a point, write
the measurement that earned it; where you withhold one, write the reason. Then review the ten and mark any
point you awarded on judgement rather than a number.

*Why this matters:* forcing a measurement behind every point turns confluence from an impression into a
record, and reviewing the scores afterwards is the only reliable way to catch the habit of adding labels
until a chart looks like a setup.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** A bullish order block runs from 1.2340 to 1.2345 and an upward fair value gap runs from
1.2348 to 1.2355. What is the correct assessment?

- A. They are confluent, because the two zones are adjacent on the chart.
- B. They overlap by three pips, so the confluence point is valid.
- C. They share no prices, so there is no overlap and the claim of confluence does not hold.
- D. The order block should be widened until it reaches the gap.

**Correct answer:** C

**Why:** Overlap is an arithmetic test rather than a visual one, and two zones that do not share any
prices are neighbours, not confluence.
