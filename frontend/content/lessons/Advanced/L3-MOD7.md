# Mitigation & Re-entries

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What mitigation means when price returns to the origin of a move, and how a zone changes once it has been revisited.
- How to tell a zone that is still valid from one that has already been used up, using written criteria rather than hope.
- How to judge whether a re-entry is a fresh decision on new evidence or an excuse to re-enter a losing idea.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Mitigation is the name given to price returning to the origin of a move. In the Market Structure tier you
learned to mark the order block — the last opposing candle before a strong displacement — and the fair
value gap, the thin area left behind when price travels quickly. The origin of a move is that area, the
place the move started from.

The word comes from the idea that a trader who filled that area originally is now offered a second chance
to trade near that price — a label for behaviour you can see, not a claim about who is trading. What you
can observe is narrower: an area where many transactions previously occurred is one where many positions
exist and many resting orders sit. Nothing is guaranteed by a return to the origin.

The practical problem is that a zone does not stay the same after it has been revisited. A zone that has
never been tested is unresolved, and still a place worth watching. Once price has traded back into it,
orders have been filled, positions adjusted, and stops placed around the area. This is what traders mean
when they say a zone has been used up, consumed, or already mitigated.

So the Advanced skill is not finding zones. It is assigning a state to each zone and updating that state
as the market touches it. Three states are enough: fresh, partially tested, and invalid. Fresh means price
has not returned since the zone formed. Partially tested means price has traded into the zone but has not
closed beyond its far edge. Invalid means price has closed beyond that far edge, at which point the
location no longer supports the reaction you were watching for.

A re-entry is a second position in the same direction, taken at a location related to a first one.
Re-entries have a poor reputation because they are the most common way a losing idea gets disguised as a
new one. The test is simple and uncomfortable: would you take this setup on a blank chart, with no open
position and no memory of the first attempt? If yes, and your written criteria are met, it is a fresh
decision. If the only reason you are taking it is that you already hold a position there, it is attachment
wearing a strategy's clothes.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose it is a London session and
you are watching EUR/USD on the fifteen-minute chart.

Price has been drifting between 1.0800 and 1.0830 for several hours. A down candle then forms covering
1.0812 to 1.0824. The next candle opens at 1.0825 and closes at 1.0895 on a much larger body, and in doing
so it takes out the prior swing high at 1.0844. Under your written definition, the down candle from 1.0812
to 1.0824 is the origin of that move and you mark it as a bullish zone. State: fresh.

Ninety minutes later price retraces, printing a fifteen-minute candle that runs from 1.0836 down to
1.0818. It has entered the zone and pierced below the midpoint, but it has not closed below 1.0812, the
far edge. Under your criteria the state changes from fresh to partially tested — nothing more.

Here is what you write before anything else happens. If a fifteen-minute candle closes below 1.0812, the
zone becomes invalid. If price instead holds above that level, the zone is still partly intact, and a
re-entry would need to satisfy the same written criteria you would apply to a zone you had never touched.

Notice what the example does not tell you. It does not say price held, and it does not say it broke
through. Either was possible. A return to the origin of a move is a situation, not a result.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Treating a zone as fresh because its rectangle is still drawn on the chart.
- Re-entering repeatedly after the zone closed beyond its far edge, calling each attempt a new setup.
- Adding to a losing position at the origin without rewriting the criteria.
- Judging a re-entry by how much you want the first position to be right, not by what the setup shows.
- Never writing down a state for each zone, so every zone looks equally valid and equally available.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Label every zone fresh, partially tested or invalid, and only ever re-enter a market you would enter from a blank chart.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing
you do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one pair and one timeframe, and find five historical moves where price displaced sharply from a
clear origin. For each, mark the origin zone and track what price did on its next approach.

Build a table with four columns: the zone boundaries, how many times price returned to it, how deep the
deepest return travelled within the zone as a percentage of the zone's height, and the final state you
assigned it — fresh, partially tested or invalid. Where a return occurred, write one sentence stating what
you would have required before treating it as a re-entry, and one stating the level at which that idea
would have been abandoned.

*Why this matters:* recording how deep each return travelled and where each zone ended up forces you to
see that a touched zone is a different object from an untouched one, and writing the abandonment level in
advance is what separates a re-entry decision from a re-entry excuse.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** You marked a bullish zone and price has since traded into it twice, each time closing back
above the zone's lower edge. What is the most accurate description of the zone?

- A. It is still fresh, because it has never closed beyond its lower edge.
- B. It is invalid, because any return to a zone destroys it.
- C. It is partially tested, so any re-entry from it must satisfy the same written criteria you would apply to a new zone.
- D. It is fresh again, because two tests cancel each other out and restore the original conditions.

**Correct answer:** C

**Why:** A zone that has been entered but not closed beyond remains partially tested, and a re-entry is
only defensible when it would be taken on the same written criteria as a setup you had never touched.
