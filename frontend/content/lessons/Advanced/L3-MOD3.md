# Smart Money Concepts (SMC)

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What the Smart Money Concepts vocabulary describes, and how its parts fit together as one framework.
- How to use that framework as a lens for reading a chart rather than a set of signals to fire.
- How to write a single market story that connects structure, liquidity, imbalance and premium or discount.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Smart Money Concepts, usually shortened to SMC, is not a separate method that replaces what you already
learned. Across the earlier tiers you met the individual pieces: market structure, liquidity, liquidity
sweeps, fair value gaps, order blocks, premium and discount, and multi-timeframe analysis. SMC is the name for the habit of arranging those pieces into one story about a
chart, instead of treating each one as an independent trigger to fire.

The framework rests on a handful of ideas, each answering a different question. Structure answers: which
way is the market moving on the layer I am trading, and which level would tell me I am wrong? Liquidity
answers: where are orders likely resting, and where would price have a mechanical reason to travel? Imbalance answers: where did price move so quickly that it left an inefficient area behind, and
where might it later return? Premium and discount answer: measured against the current range, is price
expensive or cheap, which shapes where a sensible entry sits. Order blocks — the supply and demand zones
you learned to mark in the Market Structure tier — answer: which specific area was the origin of the move
that changed structure?

Each of those is a question, not a signal. A fair value gap is not a buy order. An order block is not a
place to leave a limit order and walk away. They are facts about the chart that become meaningful only when
several of them agree on one story. For example: structure is bullish on the four-hour chart, price has
traded below a recent minor low, it left an imbalance on the way
away from that low, and it is now retracing into the cheaper half of the range at the zone that caused the
structural break. That sentence is a lens.

The temptation is to see setups everywhere: once you can name liquidity, imbalance and order blocks, every
pullback looks like an entry. The discipline is to require the pieces to line up in the same direction on
the same timeframe, and to accept that on many charts they will not.

SMC speaks of "smart money", which sounds like it names
participants who know something you do not. You cannot observe participant identity on a
candlestick chart, and nothing in this framework requires you to. Read it mechanically: liquidity is a
description of where resting orders cluster, and price movement is a description of aggressive orders
consuming them. The vocabulary describes supply and demand at specific prices, and that is all it needs to be.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to illustrate the process. Suppose it is a Wednesday in the London
session and you are studying EUR/USD on the fifteen-minute chart, with the four-hour chart read first.

On the four-hour chart you apply a swing rule of three candles on each side. Price has made higher highs and
higher lows from 1.0820 to 1.0960, and the most recent pullback held above 1.0880. External structure reads
up.

On the fifteen-minute chart you see a minor low at 1.0905 sitting just above an equal low from the previous
day at 1.0906. That cluster is the kind of place where sell-side orders rest. Price trades down to 1.0898,
below both, then closes the fifteen-minute period back at 1.0912. The candle that took the low has a large
body, and it leaves a gap between its high and the high of the candle before it — an imbalance.

You now have three of the four questions answered. Structure points up. Liquidity below 1.0905 appears to
have been interacted with. An imbalance sits above. The fourth question, discount, is what remains:
measured against the recent fifteen-minute range from 1.0898 to 1.0960, is the area just above 1.0905 in the
cheaper half? You would mark that zone, check whether it overlaps the origin of the move that took the low,
and write your invalidation below 1.0898.

Notice what this does not tell you. Price could have continued lower and invalidated the reading entirely. What you have is a lens, and a
level at which the lens stops working.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Firing a trade the moment one part appears, before the other three agree.
- Re-drawing zones after the fact so the story fits what price did.
- Running the framework on every timeframe at once, so four stories conflict.
- Treating "smart money" as people whose intentions you can read off a chart.
- Describing a setup without naming the price that proves it wrong.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Treat Smart Money Concepts as a set of questions to ask a chart in order, never as a set of signals to fire
at price.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Open the SAGZFX Practice Account. It uses virtual money only, no real order is ever placed, and nothing you
do here can earn or lose you real funds.

Choose one major pair and mark up one week of the fifteen-minute chart. On a separate note, write the four
questions of the framework as headings: structure, liquidity, imbalance, and premium or discount. Under each
heading, write one sentence that answers it for this chart, using specific prices. Then write a single
paragraph of no more than four sentences joining the four answers into one story about where price is and
what you would want to see next. Finally, state the price level at which that story stops being true.

*Why this matters:* writing the four answers separately before joining them forces you to notice when one is
missing or contradicts the others, which is exactly the moment most traders stop checking and start hoping.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** A student marks a fair value gap on the fifteen-minute chart and immediately places a limit
order to trade from it. What is the main error in framework terms?

- A. Fair value gaps are not part of the framework at all.
- B. A fair value gap is one fact, not a story, and the framework asks for structure, liquidity, imbalance
  and premium or discount to agree before the fact means anything.
- C. Fair value gaps only matter on the daily chart.
- D. Limit orders can never be used once an imbalance has been marked.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** A single piece of the vocabulary describes one thing about the chart; it becomes a lens only when
the other questions are answered in the same direction, and it is never a trigger on its own.
