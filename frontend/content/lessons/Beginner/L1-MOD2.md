# Currency pairs & market sessions

## 1. What You'll Learn

- How pairs are grouped into majors, minors or crosses, and exotics, and why the group matters.
- The four main sessions — Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York — and when each one runs.
- Why activity and spreads change from session to session, and how daylight saving shifts the clock.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

Not every currency pair behaves the same way, and one of the main reasons is which group it belongs
to. Pairs are grouped into three families, and the group tells you something about cost and
temperament before you look at a single chart.

**Majors** are the pairs that include the US dollar on one side and another heavily traded currency on
the other: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD and NZD/USD. They are the most
actively traded pairs in the world, which normally means tighter spreads and smoother price movement.

**Minors**, also called crosses, are pairs that do not include the US dollar — EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY,
GBP/JPY and similar. They are still liquid but generally cost more to trade than majors, because
fewer participants quote them directly.

**Exotics** pair a major currency with the currency of a smaller or emerging economy, such as
USD/ZAR, USD/TRY or USD/MXN. Spreads are usually much wider and price can move sharply on limited
news. They are not a beginner's market, and a wide spread is a permanent headwind on every trade you
take.

Now to timing. The forex day follows the sun through four main sessions. **Sydney** opens the week.
**Tokyo**, also called the Asian session, comes next and is typically the quietest of the four, with
price often drifting in a narrow range. **London** follows and is the largest session by volume.
**New York** runs through the second half of London, and the hours where the two overlap are usually
the most active of the entire day.

Why does this matter to you? Two reasons. First, **spreads** are typically tighter when more
participants are quoting prices, and wider during quiet hours. Second, **movement** is uneven — a
pair can spend the Asian session drifting in a small range and then travel further in the first hour
of London than it did all night. Trading a quiet session because it is convenient for your schedule
means paying more and moving less.

One practical complication: session times shift with daylight saving. London and New York both
observe it, and their clocks do not change on the same date. For you in West Africa Time, this means
the London open is not a fixed hour all year. Check the actual open and close times before you build
a routine around them.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used to show you how sessions show up on a chart. Suppose you are
looking at GBP/USD on a fifteen-minute chart on a Wednesday. You have marked the Asian session, the
London session and the New York session as vertical bands on the chart so you can see where each one
begins.

In the Asian band, price travels between 1.2640 and 1.2662 — a range of 22 pips across several hours.
The candles are small and mostly overlapping. The spread you note during this period is wider than
usual.

In the London band, the candles become noticeably larger. Between 08:00 and 10:00 London time, price
moves from 1.2655 down through 1.2640 and prints a low near 1.2608. You would also expect the spread
to have narrowed by this point.

This is a description of what the chart shows, not a signal. Notice that the Asian range being broken
does not tell you what happens next. Price could continue lower, could reverse and reclaim the range,
or could stall. A session boundary is a change in the conditions you are trading in — not a
prediction, and not an entry trigger on its own. Setups of this shape fail regularly, and any honest
description of a trade has to include the possibility of loss.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Trading whatever pair is trending on social media rather than one whose spread and behaviour you understand.
- Treating exotic pairs like majors, then discovering the spread eats most of a small move.
- Assuming session times are fixed all year and missing the London open after a daylight saving change.
- Trading the quiet Asian session on a pair that only really moves during London.
- Judging a pair's typical movement from a single day instead of watching it across a full week.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Choose the session before you choose the trade, because you are selecting the conditions you will be
trading in, not just the hour you happen to be free.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** Which of the following best describes a "minor" or cross pair?

- A. A pair containing the US dollar on one side, such as EUR/USD.
- B. A pair that does not contain the US dollar, such as EUR/GBP.
- C. A pair made up of a major currency and an emerging-market currency, such as USD/ZAR.
- D. Any pair that can only be traded during the London session.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** Minors, or crosses, are pairs that exclude the US dollar; pairs mixing a major with an emerging-market currency are exotics, and majors contain the dollar.
