# Stop Loss & Take Profit

## 1. What You'll Learn

- What a stop-loss and a take-profit actually are, and the decision each one records.
- How to choose a stop-loss level from the reason you entered rather than from the money involved.
- Why moving a stop-loss further away to avoid a loss is one of the most damaging habits a trader can form.

## 2. Plain-English Explanation

A stop-loss and a take-profit are the two prices at which you have decided a trade ends. They are not
predictions. They are decisions, written down in advance, about what you will do in each direction.

A **stop-loss** marks the planned exit if your reason for the trade turns out to be wrong. This is the
important part: the stop is tied to your **reason**, not to a round number and not to how much money
you are willing to lose. If you entered because price held above a level, then price moving back below
that level is the event that invalidates the idea, and the stop belongs on the other side of it. The
stop answers the question "what would prove me wrong?" and it is the direct expression of that answer.

A **take-profit** is a planned exit at a target. It answers a different question: "where would I
consider this idea complete?" A target is a plan, not a promise. Price may reach it, may stop short of
it, or may pass straight through it. Setting one in advance means you are not deciding in the middle
of a live trade, when the money on the screen is influencing your judgement.

Both orders are placed on the platform. Read them back before you confirm the trade, and check that
the levels are the ones you intended rather than the platform's defaults.

Now the hard part, and the single most important behavioural rule in this lesson. **Do not move a
stop-loss further away to avoid a loss.** When price approaches your stop, the temptation is strong:
widen the stop a little, give the trade room, and avoid recording the loss. This converts a small,
planned, affordable loss into an open-ended one. It also breaks the relationship between your risk
and your position size, because you sized the position based on where the stop was. Every account
that has been destroyed by a single trade was destroyed by a stop that kept moving.

There is a second detail worth knowing. A stop-loss is an instruction, not a guarantee. When your
stop triggers, it becomes a market order, and market orders can fill at a worse price than requested
— most often around major news releases or when liquidity is thin. So the loss you take can exceed the
loss you planned. Trading carries a real risk of loss, and planning for that possibility is part of
using stops honestly.

## 3. Real Chart Walkthrough

These are hypothetical numbers, used only to show you how the reasoning works. Suppose you are looking
at EUR/USD on the H1 chart during the London session. Price has been ranging between 1.0800 and 1.0860
for several days. You are watching the resistance band at 1.0860.

Your reason for interest is that price closing and holding above 1.0860 would suggest the range is no
longer containing price. So an entry above the band only makes sense with a stop that says the idea
was wrong: below the band, at a level that would mean price had fallen back into the range. You might
place the stop near 1.0830, roughly 30 pips below the entry, with the spread accounted for.

For the take-profit, you would look for a level where you would consider the idea complete. If the
range is 60 pips tall, a target 60 pips above the entry is a reasonable planning choice, though the
market owes you nothing and may never get there.

Now consider the alternative path. Price enters at 1.0860 and falls. It approaches 1.0830. Widening
the stop to 1.0800 would feel like patience. It is not patience; it is doubling the risk on a trade
whose reason has already failed. And notice that even with a stop in place, a fast move could fill
beyond 1.0830. This setup fails regularly, and any honest account of it has to include that.

## 4. Common Mistakes

- Setting a stop-loss by counting money instead of by identifying what would prove the trade wrong.
- Widening a stop when price approaches it, to avoid recording the loss.
- Trading without a stop at all, on the reasoning that the position can be watched manually.
- Choosing a take-profit so far away that it is never realistic, then holding a winner until it turns.
- Assuming the stop will always fill at exactly the level chosen, with no slippage.

## 5. The SAGZFX Rule

Place your stop-loss at the price that would prove your reason wrong, and never move it further away
once the trade is live.

## 6. Practice Assignment

Complete this exercise in the SAGZFX Practice Account, which uses virtual money only. No real order is
placed and no real funds are at risk.

Choose three historical setups on the H1 chart for EUR/USD or GBP/USD. For each one, write the
following on paper before placing anything:

1. The entry price, the session, and the timeframe.
2. Your reason for the trade, in one sentence, phrased as a condition rather than an opinion.
3. The stop-loss level, plus one sentence stating exactly what would be proven wrong if price reached it.
4. The take-profit level, plus one sentence stating what would make you consider the idea complete.
5. The distance from entry to stop in pips, and the distance from entry to target in pips.

Then place all three as demo orders with the smallest lot size available, and observe what happens to
each. Finally, write a short paragraph on any occasion where you felt the urge to widen a stop, and
what you did instead.

*Why this matters:* the stop is the trade. A trader who can state, in one sentence, what would prove
them wrong has already done most of the risk work.

## 7. Checkpoint Question

**Question:** What should determine where you place your stop-loss?

- A. The amount of money you are willing to lose on the trade.
- B. The price at which your reason for entering the trade would be proven wrong.
- C. A round number near your entry, such as the nearest 50 pips.
- D. The maximum distance your broker allows.

**Correct answer:** B

**Why:** The stop expresses the invalidation of your idea, and position size is then calculated from that distance rather than the other way around.
